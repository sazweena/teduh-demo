# Teduh (Cool Routes)

Team Simelarps, Township of Tomorrow (Planet), Sime Darby Property University Hackathon 2026.

Teduh helps township planners inspect walking heat, compare fixes within a budget, and hear from residents.

The resident phone view shows the same browser-local reports and status updates, including mapped planning entries.

## Future planning method

The Future planning view is a schematic next-phase neighbourhood linked from the Elmina map. Its concern panel reads mapped reference entries from `v2-report-references.mjs` for heat, difficult crossings, and interrupted footpaths, then shows how each issue could change a future layout. The diagram is generated from a 7 by 7 walking graph, not an actual proposed site plan or a record of resident trips. Homes and daily destinations are placed at selected graph nodes. Each ordinary graph edge represents 125 m. Walking times are rounded up from the shortest available graph route at 80 m/min (4.8 km/h); they do not account for waiting at crossings, terrain, weather, or individual walking speed. A central divider blocks intermediate crossings in the starting layout. Selecting the crossing concern adds one direct crossing edge; selecting the connection concern adds one path edge. The shade concern adds trees along the selected route and does not alter its walking time. The Base layout and Revised layout controls recalculate and redraw the same home-to-destination journey. The view does not use shared resident submissions or demonstrate a measured 15-minute access result in Elmina.

## Data and limits

The map uses an OpenStreetMap snapshot for actual Elmina street geometry. The colored planning route uses nearby mapped *Jalan Elmina Ilham 3* as a visual reference for the script's *Jalan Elmina 3*. Confirm the exact pilot route and walking access on site. Priority, shade, walkers, fix costs, and impact are scenario estimates, not field measurements.

The map-first view uses MapLibre with OpenStreetMap raster tiles. It opens on the park footpaths beside Jalan Frekuensi U16/116 and U16/119, and can tilt to a 2.5D view on the same page. The building layer now covers the Ilham and Frekuensi area. It uses mapped OpenStreetMap building polygons where available. The original 16 Frekuensi blocks beside U16/137 retain their roof inset margins: 8 m at the north edge of the north row, 3 m at its south edge, 3 m at the north edge of the south row, 2.5 m at its south edge, and 1.5 m at each side. Larger mapped blocks are divided into smaller terrace roof units. Where OSM has no individual house outlines around Jalan Elmina Ilham 3, `v2-houses.mjs` places approximate 6.7 m by 11 m roof units beside residential road centre lines. The rows and developed side of outer streets were chosen using the user-provided 9 November 2026 aerial screenshot; the map coordinates come from OSM road geometry, not from surveyed or georeferenced roof tracing. Generated roofs are deliberately omitted near mapped building polygons and road junctions. These shapes and their uniform 6 m height are for map visualization, not verified building locations or heights.

The ground-shadow layer uses NOAA's solar position equations for the current Kuala Lumpur date, selected hour, and Elmina coordinates in Malaysia time (UTC+8), then projects each roof onto a flat ground plane. Neighbouring shadow polygons can overlap and visually darken in the map renderer. It computes direct-sun building shadows only: tree canopies, overhangs, roof profiles, terrain, clouds, and buildings outside the selected area are not included. The mapped roads are centre lines; this layer does not measure shade on surveyed sidewalks or calculate a pedestrian exposure score. Solar method: [NOAA General Solar Position Calculations](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/solareqns.PDF).
The v2 street-priority overlay covers mapped residential streets and pedestrian links around Jalan Elmina Ilham 3. Its street lines come from a separate OpenStreetMap API extract dated 9 November 2026 (`v2-ilham-roads.json`), which includes Jalan Frekuensi U16/120, U16/121, and other streets missing from the older whole-town extract. Pedestrian links omitted at the north edge of that extract are supplemented by OSM footways in the 21 October 2026 whole-town extract (`elmina-map.json`), including the path through the strip beside Jalan Frekuensi U16/138. Mapped pedestrian paths have a blue line so they can be distinguished from street centre lines. Its 7 AM to 7 PM colours come from explicit planning weights in `v2-street-priority.mjs`, with the order changing by hour and issue type. These colours are relative planning priorities, not measured temperatures, shade percentages, or live heat data. Unnamed mapped links are coloured but have no added name label. Additional garden paths and actual resident walking routes require a site survey or consented GPS traces before they can be represented as known paths.

The v2 priority sidebar multiplies three factors: modelled walkers, issue condition, and walking-destination proximity. The walker count starts from a preset per-street peak and an assumed hourly pattern, then adds 8 for each mapped report card or browser-local report on that street and 4 for each supporter recorded for that street, including browser-local Me too counts and session activity. It is divided by 160 and capped at 1 for the first factor. The condition factor follows the report kind: sunlight for heat, darkness for dark reports, and fixed planning weights of 0.88 for crossing concerns or 0.85 for damaged paths; streets without a mapped concern use 0.55. The third factor automatically matches each report point with the eight walking hotspots by straight-line distance. Each hotspot contributes max(0, 1 minus distance/1500 m); the total is divided by 0.625 times the hotspot count and capped at 1. Where several reports share a street, their proximity scores are averaged and the UI counts places within 1 km of any report point. Seeded cards without an exact point use a mapped street midpoint. Streets with no report use distance from the road line. The UI shows the closest hotspot and distance. These distances are straight-line proximity, not walked route lengths, visits, or observed pedestrian flows. Mapped cards and generated session supporters are planning inputs, not independently verified submissions. Browser-local reports are counted when present but are not shared between devices. When reports have different issue kinds on one street, the newest browser-local kind controls the condition factor. The displayed walker count is a model value, not a measured count. For named routes, the previous assumed hourly walking patterns remain in use.

The budget popup accepts RM1,000 to RM25,000 for the selected street. Proceed saves that street's amount in this browser, plays a circular network animation adapted from the local IBM Datathon frontend, and shows an itemised improvement plan. Three fixed options are available: Plan A plants two young trees for RM1,000; Plan B installs one shade sail with siting allowance for RM8,000; Plan C plants eight young trees and installs one shade sail with siting allowance for RM13,000. Each option shows its advantages, drawbacks and timing, and choices above the available budget cannot be selected. The chosen option controls the included works, remaining budget, saved plan and Ilham 3 preview. The card graphics show the actual number of trees and sails in each option. These are planning allowances, not supplier quotations or an engineering bill of quantities. A raised crossing is outside this small-works budget and would need a separate road-authority design and budget.

The v2 budget result offers a 3D preview for Jalan Elmina Ilham 3 when a fixed plan is selected. The Preview changes in 3D button opens `map-3d.html?preview=ilham3` with the chosen budget and plan ID. That view starts level, tilts its camera, loads one existing intermediate-house GLB, extrudes neighbouring Ilham and Frekuensi house outlines at a uniform 6 m visual height, and draws the included trees, sail, or crossing as simple 3D objects. Two approximate roof units nearest the GLB are omitted to leave its frontage clear. The house is provisionally placed at longitude 101.51837, latitude 3.18566 with an approximate orientation; the other objects use evenly spaced road-centre interpolations and a fixed northward offset. None of these placements or the house roof outline has been verified against satellite imagery or surveyed frontage. The 3D view fills the map area without a side panel; markers can be clicked for details, and Back to map returns to the v2 map. The 3D geometry is a placement study, not an impact calculation or an approved construction layout.

The eight-tree Plan C planting mix is four Bunga Tanjung (*Mimusops elengi*), two Kayu Manis hutan (*Cinnamomum iners*), and two Bungor Raya (*Lagerstroemia speciosa*). Plan A uses one Bunga Tanjung and one Kayu Manis hutan. The 2026 [Jabatan Landskap Negara tree price schedule](https://elandskap.jln.gov.my/storage/uploads/epact/dokumen/1772691531702_JKH%202026.pdf) lists standard-tree stock prices of RM320, RM380, and RM350 respectively, totalling RM2,740 for the eight-tree mix before planting work. A 2025 [Sepang council tender schedule](https://tender.selangor.my/uploads/5NEiqwa5sgJf6Gbs883dyzQ0F3KJav2CcVqoGS8W/DOKUMEN%20SEBUT%20HARGA%20%28SH%2026%29.pdf) lists RM500 per 1–2 m shade tree supplied and planted, including site preparation, planting hole, soil and stakes. The plans use that RM500 benchmark per young tree; it does not include future maintenance or unusual pavement and utility works. The RM7,000 sail allowance is derived from [Coolaroo Malaysia's installation guide](https://coolaroomalaysia.com/catalogue/Coolaroo%20Sunshade%202024.pdf): RM1,550 for a 5 m × 3 m commercial sail, four RM680 posts, four RM500 footings, four RM45 hardware sets, RM300 installation and RM300 delivery, or RM7,050 in total, rounded to RM7,000. Siting and contingency add RM1,000 for Plan B or RM2,000 for Plan C. The supplier guide excludes breaking existing concrete and quotes taller posts separately. Species suitability, sail structure, foundation, permissions, and actual procurement prices require site and supplier review. The same three fixed options appear for each selected street; they are not a street-specific optimisation from measured conditions.

The Resident routes layer is shown by default and draws four repeatable GPS-like lines around each selected OSM footpath beside Jalan Frekuensi U16/138 and U16/110 and through the park loop near U16/116 and U16/119. `v2-resident-routes.mjs` samples the centre line every 3 m, estimates the local tangent from points 2 m ahead and behind, and displaces each sample along the perpendicular with two smooth sine waves plus a small lane offset. The displacement tapers to zero at the ends so mapped junctions stay aligned. Eight lighter, generated shortcut traces cross between points on the mapped loop near the play area. The lines are generated visual route patterns, not recorded trips, resident counts, or evidence of footpath access. The winding interior garden paths visible in satellite imagery are not digitized from Google imagery.

Selecting a red priority pin or its offscreen indicator opens the street detail panel. The selected location moves to the center of the remaining visible map area, to the left of the panel on desktop and above it on narrow screens. The same camera placement works on the Street and Satellite basemaps.

At 7:00 AM and 7:00 PM, the map displays long directional shadows at 50% opacity. It uses the calculated solar azimuth and a minimum 5 degree elevation at these two endpoints to keep the shapes visible. If the calculated sun is below the horizon, this endpoint display is a low-sun visualization rather than a physical direct-sun shadow. From 8:00 AM through 6:00 PM, the shadows use the calculated elevation without this floor and appear at 76% opacity.

Sky tone is applied by default to the OpenStreetMap raster for the selected hour: cool and dim at 7 AM, gently warm at 8-9 AM, neutral from 10 AM through 3 PM, increasingly golden at 4-6 PM, and cool and dim at 7 PM. It changes map color only, not sun position, shadows, street priority, or Google satellite imagery. The palette follows general photography guidance on golden-hour warmth and blue-hour cool tones; it does not represent measured sky or weather conditions. Sources: [Adobe on golden and blue hour](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/photography/technique/golden-hour.html), [Nikon on early and late light](https://www.nikonusa.com/learn-and-explore/c/tips-and-techniques/tips-for-photographing-mountains), [MapLibre raster paint properties](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/).

The v2 map starts with the OpenStreetMap Street map. Its optional Satellite switch uses Google Maps JavaScript satellite imagery on localhost, with the OpenStreetMap road and selected building footprints and the same calculated shadows drawn above it. Satellite images were captured on dates independent of the selected shadow time, so shadows already visible in the photograph may differ. The Street map remains the MapLibre view with the 2.5D tilt; Google satellite is top-down here. Google supplies the imagery and its attribution, while the road and building geometry comes from the OpenStreetMap snapshot. The local server reads `TEDUH_GOOGLE_MAPS_KEY` from the Windows user environment and never writes it to the repository. A Maps Demo Key supports this local Google Maps JavaScript view but not Map Tiles API.

The Walking Hotspots toggle shows the same eight custom markers in Street and Satellite views: [Laman Nadi shops](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/912610185), [Surau Elmina Gardens](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/911499103), [My Health Clinic @ Elmina](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/12001743462), [the park by U16/119](https://www.openstreetmap.org/?mlat=3.18847&mlon=101.51920#map=18/3.18847/101.51920), [the park by U16/118](https://www.openstreetmap.org/?mlat=3.18743&mlon=101.52140#map=18/3.18743/101.52140), [Elmina Urban Park](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1309457596), [Elmina Lakeside Mall](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1308938137), and Jaya Grocer from the project's 2026-10-21 OpenStreetMap extract (`elmina-map.json`). The two central park pins are representative points matched to the green spaces in the supplied map screenshot; their names describe nearby streets rather than official park names. Coordinates of other mapped areas and buildings are feature centroids or representative points, not surveyed entrances. These places identify potential walking destinations and contribute to the third priority factor through straight-line proximity. They do not measure visits or foot traffic. The markers are stored in `v2-destinations.mjs`; the toggle changes their visibility, not the calculation. Google supplies only the Satellite basemap, not the place data.

The 3D view uses MapLibre and OpenFreeMap vector tiles for the basemap. Outside the Ilham and Frekuensi area, building extrusions use the project's OpenStreetMap footprint snapshot with a uniform 5 m visual height because that snapshot has no building heights. In the Ilham and Frekuensi area, the mapped and road-aligned approximate roof units from `v2-houses.mjs` are extruded at 6 m; their source footprints are omitted to prevent stacked geometry. Five adjacent mapped terrace footprints near Jalan Metafasa U16/163 use original GLB house models, one end unit and four intermediate units. Their facades, side yard, and heights are visual approximations, not verified designs of those homes or of homes on Jalan Elmina Ilham 3. The models are scaled to the selected footprints and replace their generic extrusions. Modular sidewalk GLBs follow those five frontages, with tree-well modules at alternating houses and a side-yard extension at the end unit. Straight road GLBs follow the nearby mapped road centerline. Their 3 m sidewalk and 6 m road widths are visual assumptions; OpenStreetMap does not provide surveyed curb positions here. A visual verge with separate driveway strips fills the gap between the modeled sidewalk and mapped road strip; its layout is not surveyed. Intersections and bends still use the basemap. Trees, sails, crossings, and lights are georeferenced representations of saved proposed fixes, not surveyed designs. The view flags saved points that overlap mapped building footprints; review those placements before using them in a plan. The 3D view reads the same browser-local proposed fixes and reports as the 2D map and refreshes while open.

This GitHub Pages site has no backend. Proposed map fixes and resident reports are stored in the current browser and can appear across the 2D and 3D views. New reports from `sdp-app.html` are saved through `static-api.js` and appear in the map-first developer Reports panel on the same browser. The resident page reads developer status and reply changes on refresh or when its tab regains focus. Neither flow submits to Sime Darby Property or a council. The separate private pilot can use a shared server and database.

The map-first workspace has Proposed changes and Reports panels over the map. Saving a budget result stores the included works as a numbered street proposal in this browser (`teduh-v2-plans-v1`), with suggested points along the selected mapped street. A confirmation opens over the map, with a button to view the saved proposal in Proposed changes. The planner can adjust the budget, inspect each work, set its map location, change its status, or remove the plan. The site-check allowance has no pin. These plans are separate from the older map's `teduh-team-local-v1` proposal records and do not automatically appear in the 3D view. The Reports panel reads the same browser-local report store as the owner-app phone page, shows map pins and status filters, and can save a status and reply. The browser-local API accepts report coordinates across the Ilham and Frekuensi map area, east to longitude 101.5221. None of these records is shared across devices.

The Reports panel includes six mapped planning entries and one unmatched entry carried over from the former planner. Seeded cards use generated photo-style illustrations, now labelled as illustrations in the report panel. They are not site photos or resident submissions. Photos actually attached through the resident form are labelled "Resident photo". New reports without an attached photo use a category-based reference image; uncategorized reports use a neutral street image. Their status and team notes can be updated in this browser (`teduh-v2-legacy-report-updates-v1`). Saving a proposed plan for a mapped street also marks its mapped entry Planned, while preserving any entry already marked Built. The shade entry appears as a report-style marker on Jalan Elmina Ilham 3 and below that street's priority formula, but its exact point is unconfirmed. Its card contributes one report count to the modelled walker input. The user attributed the Ilham 3 entry to Ms. Arifah Hasidin. No verified support count or submission date is supplied for these map cards. Session activity on the map still starts with sample support counts of 7, 18, 15, 10, 7, and 8 respectively; those values are planning inputs, not resident submissions. The resident page instead records support for each mapped entry in `teduh-v2-resident-support-v1`; the developer Reports panel shows those browser-local support counts. The crossing entry retains its earlier Persiaran Elmina name without a map pin because that location has not been matched to the current Ilham and Frekuensi view. The older planner's support counts, dates, and Planned/Built claims are not imported as resident activity. A new browser also receives one proposed RM15,000 plan on Jalan Frekuensi U16/120, with RM13,000 in included works and RM2,000 unallocated. Its suggested pins require site review. The plan is added once and is not restored after removal. The untouched older seeded RM110,000 plan is migrated to this package; other browser-local proposals keep their saved amounts until edited.

The map also shows report markers on Jalan Frekuensi U16/113 and U16/120 and Jalan Elmina Ilham 2, 4, and 5. Their notes cover shade, crossing access, and path condition; each mapped card contributes one report count to the modelled walker input. These five new descriptions and illustrations were created for this workspace, and the underlying resident submissions have not been imported. Marker positions come from mapped street geometry rather than verified issue locations.

The map also runs clearly labelled illustrative activity while the page is visible. It selects a mapped concern and fictional first name every 8 seconds for three visible notifications, then waits 15 seconds before repeating, adds one session-only supporter to its sample starting count and adds 4 to that street's modelled walker count, and shows a notification for five seconds. The displayed walker factor is capped at 1.00. These events are generated in the browser, are not resident submissions, and are not shared or persisted. The notification sound can play after a user interaction, subject to browser audio restrictions.

The dark street-priority panel ranks named mapped streets by the same score shown in each street's formula. Each bar spans 0 to 1, so its length represents the absolute score rather than a share of the highest-ranked street. The list reorders with the time slider. Ilham 3 and Frekuensi U16/113 and U16/120 have distinct assumed hourly walking profiles in `v2-street-priority.mjs`; the other streets use the common hourly profile. The edge badges appear only after their associated map pins leave the viewport.

Serve the repository through a local HTTP server when testing. Opening the HTML files through `file://` prevents map data requests from loading. No build step is required.

## Team workflow

Pull the latest `main` before editing, test the changed screen and connected flow, then commit and push. Keep the page, script, and presentation values aligned. The site is published from this repository through GitHub Pages.
